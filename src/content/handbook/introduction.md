
# Introduction   

{{gap}}

RavenLore is a role playing game. You imagine a person, and control them through adventures of one kind or another. Usually you play with a group of 2-5 other players, who each control their own character. Together you face whatever challenges your Games Master (GM) dreams up for you. There are many rules that govern what you can do, and they are included in this book. At heart though, the game is an exercise in improvisation. The GM will set the scene, and end with the key question of any role playing game. “What do you do?”

The first step to playing RavenLore is to create a character. It should be someone you will enjoy being. Maybe you enjoy cutting goblins in half with a huge axe. Maybe you want to protect your party members from harm, tackling the toughest enemies with a sword and shield. Or a gifted archer with a terrible gambling addiction and a string of outstanding debts. A devout specialist in infiltration who performs secret missions for the church, while hunting the one armed man that killed his parents. Whatever you decide, in order to bring that idea into focus, we start by rolling your attributes. 

# CORE ATTRIBUTES:

Every character has 6 core attributes. 
The Character Creation Wizard in the Ravenlore web app  will guide you through rolling these attributes. 
What follows is a detailed guide to the benefits of each.

{{cols}}
### Strength

Strength (Str) measures a character’s brute muscle power and their ability to use it. A high Strength provides extra damage on every hit. This is extremely important in close combat especially against good Armor. Arguably the most important stat for warriors. Strength checks are taken for things like breaking free of a monster's grasp. 

{{attrTable:strength}}

{{gap}}

**Damage Bonus:** Added to your damage with most weapons. Doubled if you are wielding a two handed weapon and buy certain skills.

{{gap}}

### Constitution

Constitution (Con) measures endurance, fitness, health and resistance to hardship and disease. This determines how much damage you can take before you die. Constitution checks are taken for things like resisting poison.

{{attrTable:constitution}}

{{gap}}

**Torso Hit Points:** This is how many [[hit points]] you have in your Torso. You won’t be getting any more.

**Weight Allowance:** This is the amount of weight you can comfortably carry all day. Your strength is added to this number. 

{{gap}}

### Willpower

Willpower (WP) describes your determination, self control, bravery and resistance to magical influence. Willpower checks are taken for resisting some spells, and for controlling yourself against things like fear. 

{{attrTable:willpower}}

**Arcane Power:** This is your raw aptitude for magic. Arcane power is important for spellcasters, especially at low level.

{{gap}}

{{col}}

### Dexterity

Dexterity (Dex) measures a character's coordination and reflexes. Dexterity limits how good a character can become with some skills, and is essential for combat both close and ranged weapons. The bonus expertise makes you hit more often, and get hit less often. Dexterity checks are taken for things like a tricky jump, or dodging.

{{attrTable:dexterity}}

**Expertise Bonus:88 This is added to your [[combat rolls]]. This will help you get more chances to do damage.

**Initiative Bonus:** This is added to all your [[initiative]] rolls.

**Precision Bonus:** This is added to your [[Targeting Rolls]].  This will help you hit weak points and avoid shields.

{{gap}}
### Awareness

Awareness (Aw) measures how present in the moment you are and your ability to focus usefully. It limits more Trades & Talents than any other attribute. Awareness checks are taken for things like noticing a hidden enemy or finding a secret door. 

{{attrTable:awareness}}

**Skill Cap** (SC): in the general skill table, the maximum of many skills is listed as SC. This is your maximum base percentage chance to succeed with those skills.

**Evasion Bonus:** This is subtracted from Targeting Rolls against you.


### Charisma

Charisma
Charisma (Chr) measures charm, social skill, and attractiveness of all kinds. Danny Devito would have a very high charisma. Charisma checks are taken for things like a guard’s reaction to you.

# PROFESSIONS

RavenLore does not use a class system. All of the abilities and powers in the game are available to anyone who can meet the prerequisites, find an appropriate trainer, and who spends the skill points on it. What combination of skills you decide to develop is up to you. Most characters though do have a profession. What does your character do for a living? In the following chapter you will find several lists of Skills, all of which must be paid for from the same pool of skill points. As you build your character, think about what you want to be best at, and what skills will help you achieve that.  Before you start spending Skill points, you’ll need a better idea of how play actually works:

Let’s start with an example.
